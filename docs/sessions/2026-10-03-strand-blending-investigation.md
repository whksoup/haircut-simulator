# 2026-10-03 — Strand blending investigation

## Purpose and scope

Investigate why selected seam permeability has little visible effect and why adjacent hair planes remain separated when fully blended. Scope v1 is diagnosis; no rendering behavior has been changed. Owner: primary agent. Source: `F:/haircut-simulator`, HEAD `c153e5f`; tracked source was clean. ## Findings and evidence

- The control is connected: `ui.js` calls `SeamTool.setPermeability`, its edit callback calls the shared `seamsChanged`, and `GpuHairR3.syncSeams` invalidates the field, rebinds and commits buffers. This is source tracing, not a fresh pointer/UI test.
- **Free bypass of selected seams:** `SeamField` minimizes only seam penalties. Every open edge costs zero, irrespective of distance traveled. A three-facet cycle (0–1, 1–2, 2–0, unit edge lengths) with only edge 0–1 edited gives `detour(0,1) = 0` at permeability 1, 0.5 and 0. A closed barrier can work, but a local seam can be bypassed without attenuation. The search radius limits the traversal region; it does not charge for the route length inside it.
- **Open seams do not disable inferred parts:** binding rejects candidates whose initial flow disagrees with the nearest guide (`flowGate = -0.2`). Two equal-length guides at x = -1 and +1, normals +Z, tangents +X, with straight normalized shapes pointing +X and -X, give exclusive weight to opposite guides at strand x = -0.00001 and +0.00001. No authored seams are needed. Thus permeability 1 does not guarantee continuous blending.
- **The actual head has split normals:** read POSITION, NORMAL and _FACET accessors directly from `public/models/head.glb`, selected the largest primitive, grouped positions rounded to five decimal places, and compared different-facet normals at coincident vertices. Of 3010 pair comparisons, 3008 differ by more than 0.1 degrees. Within the startup hair facets 308/309/329, all four comparisons differ, with maximum angle 39.006933 degrees. These are pair comparisons, not unique boundaries. The sampler interpolates each triangle's imported normals; it does not weld/smooth them across facets. The shader lifts the blended shape through that strand normal, so this discontinuity survives fully open seam weights.
- **Additional reconstruction concern:** the texture stores each guide's normalized coordinates in its own frame, but the shader averages those coordinates directly and applies one blended strand frame afterward. This is not equivalent to blending guide displacements expressed in a common coordinate frame. Binding also chooses tangent signs relative to the dominant guide. Comb can update authored tangents while `setGuides` uploads only points/length/rate, leaving the bound tangent stale. These are source findings; their visual contribution has not yet been isolated.
- Three-nearest selection can introduce additional discontinuities where a contributing guide is replaced with nonzero weight; the grid search stops after finding three candidates rather than proving they are the nearest. A complete continuity fix must evaluate this too.

## Scope v2 implementation and checkpoints

- Derived GPU xyz now stores each guide's mesh-local displacement, including its own frame and length. Authored normalized points, roots and JSON schemas are unchanged. Frame edits travel through the same row upload as points; no comb-time rebind.
- Removed the inferred flow gate and sampled-normal weighting from binding. Exact nearest-four search supplies three compactly weighted rows; the third weight fades to zero at ordinary neighbor exchanges. Root sampling/seeds are unchanged. Exact four-way equidistance remains a singularity of fixed three-guide support; ties are finite and deterministic, not a general continuity guarantee.
- Seam transport subtracts open-graph distance from crossing-weighted graph distance. Open edges now charge travel, so bypassing a local seam is no longer free. Reduced default crossing scale from 3 to 1 after real-head GPU checks showed half and hard saturating at the same bypass cost with scale 3. Both startup boundaries now distinguish 0, 0.5 and 1.
